Problems I solve

Signs that traceability or the process needs order.

  • Mock recalls take hoursOr end without closing the material balance.
  • Inconsistent lot codingEach area identifies lots its own way and the chain breaks between receiving, processing and packaging.
  • Holds with no dispositionProduct on hold with no owner, no decision date and no clear record of what happened to it.
  • Losses not measured by causeScrap and rework that show up in cost but not in a KPI.
  • Hand-built reportsHours every week copying data between files for a report that arrives late.
  • Improvements that fadeProjects that start strong and fade without follow-up.

Scope and deliverables

What is included

  • Mapping of material flow and lot information, from receiving to shipping.
  • Timed one-step-back and one-step-forward traceability exercise with a material balance.
  • Review of lot coding and lot records at each stage.
  • Hold management: logging, owner, criteria and disposition date.
  • KPIs for nonconformances, holds, scrap, complaints and OEE where it applies, in Excel, SharePoint or Power BI.
  • Improvement projects with PDCA and a RACI matrix.

What you get

  1. Lot flow map showing where information gets lost.
  2. Mock recall report: time taken, product located and gaps found.
  3. Updated traceability and hold management procedures.
  4. KPI dashboard with documented measures.
  5. PDCA plan with a RACI matrix and 30/60/90-day follow-up.

About the FSMA traceability rule (section 204). If any of your products are on the FDA Food Traceability List, I help you identify the critical tracking events (CTEs) and key data elements (KDEs) you already record and the ones that are missing. The final interpretation of how the rule applies to your company belongs to your team and, if needed, your legal counsel.

How I work

Assessment, priorities, implementation and verification. Always in that order.

  1. Assessment

    I follow a real lot from start to finish and measure how long the plant takes to rebuild its history.

  2. Priorities

    I find where the information chain breaks and where the most product is lost, and we choose where to start.

  3. Implementation

    We adjust coding, forms, procedures and KPIs with a PDCA plan and owners set in a RACI matrix.

  4. Verification

    We rerun the exercise and review the KPIs at 30, 60 and 90 days to confirm the improvement holds.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to buy traceability software?

Not necessarily. First we organize the process and the records you already have. If a system makes sense later, you will know what it needs to do before you buy it.

How often should we run a mock recall?

It depends on your plan, your customer and your certification scheme; many schemes require at least one a year. It is most useful with a real, timed lot.

Which KPIs do you recommend?

A few well-chosen ones: nonconformances, open holds and their age, scrap by cause and complaints. We define them around what your management needs to decide.

Do you work with Power BI?

Yes. I can build Power BI dashboards fed from Excel or SharePoint, or keep everything in Excel if that is what your team uses.

How soon will we see results?

Follow-up is scheduled at 30, 60 and 90 days. I do not promise improvement figures before the assessment: targets are set with your own data.
